using stride or CFS the granularity really is the scheduler's clock
tick. At every tick if the dynamics of the priority queue changes you
will get kicked out.


Now while its possible to tailor the clock tick to account for context
switch time for the target resource this overlooks the fact that some
tasks may have their own load time. The basic stride scheduling
mechanism works fine if all tasks have the same conceptual load time.


Problem Statement:
I am a task with a certain priority(read #tickets). I m making a
concious decision that whenever I get a slice I want `n' Q slices
together (where Q = 1 quantum or 1 slice). If by doing so the
frequency of scheduling decreases for me then so be it.


Solution:
This is easy to accomplish using stride (and CFS). Just give him what
he wants viz., when he gets scheduled stop the timer and dont
reschedule until `n' timeslices. After which he ll get punished
naturally with n * #tickets getting subtracted from his score so he ll
fall back and others will get a chance. Whats more if you want to
discourage this practise then you can add a penalty `m' Q get
subtracted out of his score everytime he runs.
 where:
 n  m and #tasks need to have a correlation(havent thought about it
 yet)

Dont worry neither of the above two will cause starvation since the
task will move towards the front of the queue after the punishment at
a rate proportional to the #tickets he holds.
