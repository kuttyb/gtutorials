you can have it layered where the first layer is a sorted index that
points to different layers.

each layer represents a singular priority. whcih means they all get
added/subtracted by the same amount.


so instead of doing add/deletes at ln(n) where n is the total #tasks
you now have to do ln(m) on the first queue when the current tasks'
weight is higher than the front of the queue. Since its an index queue
this value should be much lower than n.

for the inner queues with lower #tasks adding/deleting to the queue is
faster and in no way representative of `n'. 

Inner queues need to be frequency distributed on priority.
